1928年3月12日：聖法蘭西斯水壩March 12, 1928: St. Francis Dam

圣弗朗西斯水坝（英语：St.The St.

圣弗朗西斯水坝（英语：St. Francis Dam），又称圣弗朗西斯基托坝（英语：San Francisquito Dam），是一座位于美国加利福尼亚州洛杉矶县北部圣弗朗西斯基托峡谷的重力拱坝，建造期间为1924年至1926年。 1928年，该坝发生溃坝事故，洪水造成至少431人死亡， 被认为是20世纪美国最严重的土木工程灾难之一，也是加利福尼亚州历史上造成第三大生命损失的事件。

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「1928年3月12日：聖法蘭西斯水壩」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

The St. Francis Dam, or the San Francisquito Dam, was a concrete gravity-arch dam located in San Francisquito Canyon in northern Los Angeles County, California, United States, that was built between 1924 and 1926. The dam failed catastrophically in 1928, killing at least 431 people in the subsequent flood, in what is considered to have been one of the worst American civil engineering disasters of the 20th century and the third-greatest loss of life in California history. The dam was built to serve the growing water needs of the city of Los Angeles, creating a large regulating and storage reservoir that was an integral part of the Los Angeles Aqueduct. It was located in San Francisquito Canyon of the Sierra Pelona Mountains, about 40 miles (64 km) northwest of downtown Los Angeles, and approximately 10 miles (16 km) north of the present day city of Santa Clarita.

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

水坝建造的目的是满足洛杉矶不断增长的用水需求，形成大型调节与储水水库，并作为洛杉矶引水渠的重要组成部分。坝址位于内华达狮子山内的圣弗朗西斯基托峡谷，距洛杉矶市中心约40英里（64千米），距今的圣塔克拉利塔约10英里（16千米）。然而，由于地基条件不佳及设计缺陷，水坝于完工仅两年后即崩溃。此次溃坝事故终结了威廉·穆赫兰的职业生涯，他当时担任洛杉矶水务与供应局（今洛杉矶水电局）的总经理与总工程师。 在洛杉矶历史的早期阶段，该市的供水主要来自洛杉矶河，水源透过名为“赞加”的一系列沟渠引流。当时，一家名为洛杉矶城市水公司（Los Angeles City Water Company）的私人企业负责运营市政供水系统。1878年，威廉·穆赫兰以“沟渠监工”（zanjero）身份受雇，他展现出卓越能力，并在完成日常工作后自学数学、水力学与地质学，逐步掌握工程相关知识。穆赫兰在公司内迅速升迁，于1886年晋升为总监。 1902年，洛杉矶市终止与水公司的租约，接管城市供水系统。洛杉矶市议会成立水务局，并于1911年改名为“水务与供应局”（Bureau of Water Works and Supply），穆赫兰同时被任命为总监与总工程师。

However, a defective soil foundation and design flaws led to the dam's collapse just two years after its completion. Its failure ended the career of William Mulholland, the general manager and chief engineer of the Bureau of Water Works and Supply (now the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power). In the early years of Los Angeles, the city's water supply was obtained from the Los Angeles River. This was accomplished by diverting water from the river through a series of ditches called zanjas. At that time, a private water company, the Los Angeles City Water Company, leased the city's waterworks and provided water to the city. Hired in 1878 as a zanjero (ditch tender), William Mulholland proved to be a brilliant employee who, after doing his day's work, would study textbooks on mathematics, hydraulics and geology, thereby teaching himself geology and engineering. Mulholland quickly moved up the ranks of the water company and was promoted to superintendent in 1886. In 1902, the City of Los Angeles ended its lease with the water company and took control over the city's water supply. The Los Angeles City Council established the Water Department – renamed the Bureau of Water Works and Supply in 1911 – with Mulholland as its superintendent. Mulholland was concurrently named as the Bureau's chief engineer following the organization's name change.

第 2 章Chapter 2

现场The event

穆赫兰在工程界声誉鹊起，主要来自他主持设计与建造的洛杉矶引水渠，当时为世界上最长的引水道。该系统仅凭重力，将水自欧文斯谷输送233英里（375千米）至洛杉矶。 工程于1913年如期完工，且成本低于预算，尽管早期曾遭欧文斯谷居民发起破坏行动。此后，引水道运作稳定，至今仍为洛杉矶主要水源之一。 在建造输水道过程中，威廉·穆赫兰首次将圣弗朗西斯基托峡谷部分地区视为潜在坝址。他认为应建造一座水库，以确保即便输水道因干旱或地震受损，也能长期供应洛杉矶所需水量。

Mulholland achieved great recognition among members of the engineering community when he supervised the design and construction of the Los Angeles Aqueduct, which at the time was the longest aqueduct in the world. It used gravity alone to bring the water 233 miles (375 km) from the Owens Valley to Los Angeles. The project was completed in 1913, on time and under budget, despite several setbacks. Excluding incidents of sabotage by Owens Valley residents during the California Water Wars, the aqueduct has continued to operate well throughout its history and remains in operation today. It was during the process of building the aqueduct that Mulholland first considered sections of San Francisquito Canyon as a potential dam site. He felt that there should be a reservoir of sufficient size to provide water for Los Angeles for an extended period in the event of a drought or if the aqueduct were damaged by an earthquake. In particular, he favored the area between where the hydroelectric power plants Powerhouses No. 1 and No. 2 were to be built, with what he perceived as favorable topography, a natural narrowing of the canyon downstream of a wide, upstream platform which would allow the creation of a large reservoir area with a minimum possible dam. A large camp had been set up to house the workers near this area, and Mulholland used his spare time becoming familiar with the area's geological features.

第 3 章Chapter 3

余波Aftermath

穆赫兰尤其看好位于水力发电厂第1号与第2号之间的区域，因为峡谷下游自然收窄，上游则有宽广平台，可用最小坝体形成大面积蓄水区。 当地设有大型工人营地，穆赫兰经常在闲暇时勘察地质特征。 在后来建坝地区，穆赫兰发现西侧山坡中上部主要由红色砾岩与砂岩组成，间杂细小石膏脉。红色砾岩下方、越过峡谷底部并延伸至东侧山壁的区域，则主要由含云母片岩的岩层构成，层理明显、断层错综且夹杂滑石。地质学家对两种地层接触带位置意见不一，但多数认为位于已不活动的圣弗朗西斯基托断层线上。穆赫兰命令在红色砾岩山坡开挖探测隧道与竖井，并进行渗水试验，结果显示山体足以支撑坝体。

In the area where the dam would later be situated, Mulholland found the mid- and upper portion of the western hillside consisted mainly of a reddish-colored conglomerate and sandstone formation that had small veins of gypsum interspersed within it. Below the red conglomerate, down the remaining portion of the western hillside, crossing the canyon floor and up the eastern wall, a drastically different rock composition prevailed. These areas were made up of mica schist that was severely laminated, cross-faulted in many areas, and interspersed with talc. Later, although many geologists disagreed on the exact location of the area of contact between the two formations, a majority opinion placed it at the inactive San Francisquito Fault line. Mulholland ordered exploratory tunnels and shafts excavated into the red conglomerate hillside to determine its characteristics and had water percolation tests performed. The results convinced him that the hill would make a satisfactory abutment for a dam should the need ever arise. A surprising aspect of the early geologic exploration came later, when the need for a dam arose. Although Mulholland wrote of the unstable nature of the face of schist on the eastern side of the canyon in his annual report to the Board of Public Works in 1911, this fact was either misjudged or ignored by Stanley Dunham, the construction supervisor of the St. Francis Dam.
