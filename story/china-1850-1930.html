中国，1850–1930China, 1850–1930

镜头里的中国，和镜头站的位置China through the lens — and where the lens stood

中国不是被“发现”的。从 1860 年代起，它被不同位置的镜头反复记录：有中国摄影师，也有随军、传教、经商的外国人。

看这些照片，既要看画面里的中国，也要看镜头站在哪里。这个专题选 12 帧，从大沽炮台到南京路，把“被拍下的中国”和“拍摄者的位置”放在同一页上。

China was not “discovered”. From the 1860s it was recorded again and again by lenses in different positions: Chinese photographers, and foreigners accompanying armies, missions and trade.

Looking at these photographs means seeing both the China inside the frame and where the camera stood. This story selects 12 images, from the Taku Forts to Nanjing Road, putting “China photographed” and “the photographer's position” on the same page.

第 1 章Chapter 1

口岸Treaty ports

1860 年，大沽炮台被攻入；同年，广州和上海的商业街已经出现在镜头里。战争与日常不是两件事，它们在同一条海岸线上发生。

In 1860 the Taku Forts were stormed; the same year, commercial streets in Guangzhou and Shanghai had already appeared on film. War and daily life were not two separate things; they happened on the same coastline.

第 2 章Chapter 2

城墙与桥梁Walls and bridges

从长城、玉带桥到朝阳门，摄影师记录的不只是建筑，也是他们进入中国的方式：随军、传教、经商、旅行。镜头的位置，决定了画面里谁能站到中间。

From the Great Wall and the Jade Belt Bridge to Chaoyangmen, photographers recorded not only buildings but the way they entered China: with armies, missions, trade and travel. The position of the lens decided who could stand in the middle of the frame.

第 3 章Chapter 3

城市The city

1920–1930 年代，上海的外滩和南京路成了现代城市的橱窗；照片也开始被印成明信片，进入消费。中国不再只是被记录的对象，也成为影像市场的一部分。

In the 1920s and 1930s, the Bund and Nanjing Road in Shanghai became shopwindows of the modern city; photographs were printed as postcards and entered the market. China was no longer only an object of recording; it was part of the image economy.
