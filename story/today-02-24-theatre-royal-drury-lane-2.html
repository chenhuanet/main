德鲁里巷皇家剧院（英语：Theatre Royal, Drury Lane，通称：Drury Lane）是伦敦科文特花园的一所西区剧院，属于一级登录建筑。剧院面朝凯瑟琳街背朝德鲁里巷。现在的建筑并不是最初的样子，而是经过了三次重建之后的结果，最早的建筑历史可追溯到1663年，若还存在就是伦敦最古老的剧院。 历史上大部分时期，它是特许剧院，专门上演规定种类的戏剧。 1660年代早期托马斯·基利格鲁下令建造这所剧院，雇佣了包括妮尔·格温在内的一批当时著名演员来定期出演剧目。1672年失火，于是基利格鲁又在其基础上重建了更大的一所剧院，1674年开张。这第二栋建筑伫立了将近120年，经任院长为柯莱·西伯、大卫·加里克、理查德·布林斯利·谢立丹。

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「1809年2月24日：皇家劇院 (德魯里巷)」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

The Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, commonly known as Drury Lane, is a West End theatre and Grade I listed building in Covent Garden, London, England. The building faces Catherine Street (earlier named Bridges or Brydges Street) and backs onto Drury Lane. The present building, opened in 1812, is the most recent of four theatres that have stood at the location since 1663, making it the oldest theatre site in London still in use. According to the author Peter Thomson, for its first two centuries, Drury Lane could "reasonably have claimed to be London's leading theatre". For most of that time, it was one of a handful of patent London theatres, granted monopoly rights to the production of "legitimate" drama (meaning spoken plays, rather than opera, dance, concerts, or plays with music).

In 1791, under Sheridan's management, the building was demolished to make way for a larger theatre which opened in 1794. This new Drury Lane survived for 15 years before burning down in 1809. The building that stands today opened in 1812. It has been the residency of well known actors including Edmund Kean, comedian Dan Leno and the musical composer and performer Ivor Novello. From the Second World War, the theatre has primarily hosted long runs of musicals, including Oklahoma!, My Fair Lady, 42nd Street and Miss Saigon, the theatre's longest-running show. The theatre is owned by the composer Andrew Lloyd Webber. Since January 2019, the venue has had ongoing renovations, and in July 2021, it reopened after over two years of extensive work and closures related to the COVID-19 pandemic. Disney's Frozen made its West End debut at Drury Lane the same year. First theatre: Theatre Royal, Bridges Street (1663) A playhouse known as the Cockpit Theatre used by Queen Anne's Men on Drury Lane was targeted by rioting apprentices on 4 March 1617. After the eleven-year-long Puritan Interregnum, which had seen the banning of pastimes regarded as frivolous, such as theatre, the English monarchy was restored to the throne with the return of Charles II in 1660. Soon after, Charles issued Letters Patent to two parties licensing the formation of new acting companies. One of these went to Thomas Killigrew, whose company became known as the King's Company, and who built a new theatre in Drury Lane.

现场The event

Dobbs, Brian. Drury Lane: Three Centuries of the Theatre Royal, 1663–1971. Cassell. 1972. Earl, John; Sell, Michael. Guide to British Theatres 1750–1950. Theatres Trust. 2000: 107–8. ISBN 0-7136-5688-3.

The King's Company was forced to commission the technically advanced and expensive Theatre Royal playhouse by the success of the rival Duke's Company, which was drawing fascinated crowds with their "moveable" or "changeable" scenery and visually gorgeous productions at the former Lisle's Tennis Court at Lincoln's Inn Fields. Imitating the innovations at Lincoln's Inn Fields, the Theatre Royal also featured moveable scenery with wings or shutters that could be smoothly changed between or even within acts. When not in use, the shutters rested out of sight behind the sides of the proscenium arch, which also served as a visual frame for the on-stage happenings. The picture-frame-like separation between audience and performance was a new phenomenon in English theatre, though it had been found on the Continent earlier. Theatre design in London remained ambivalent about the merits of the "picture-box" stage, and for many decades to come, London theatres including Drury Lane had large forestages protruding beyond the arch, often including the thrust stages found in the Elizabethan theatres. The players could still step forward and bridge the distance between performer and audience, and in addition, it was not unusual for audience members to mount the stage themselves.

ISBN 0-19-211546-4. 档案补记：德鲁里巷皇家剧院（Theatre Royal, Drury Lane），俗称特鲁里巷，是英国伦敦考文特花园的一座西区剧院，属一级保护建筑。该建筑面向凯瑟琳街（以前称为布里奇斯街或布里奇斯街），背对德鲁里巷。目前的建筑于 1812 年启用，是自 1663 年以来一直矗立于此的四座剧院中最新的一座，使其成为伦敦仍在使用的最古老的剧院遗址。根据作家彼得·汤姆森 (Peter Thomson) 的说法，在最初的两个世纪里，德鲁里巷“可以合理地声称自己是伦敦领先的剧院”。在大部分时间里，它是伦敦少数几家专利剧院之一，授予“合法”戏剧（指的是口语戏剧，而不是歌剧、舞蹈、音乐会或音乐戏剧）制作的垄断权。该遗址上的第一座剧院是应托马斯·基利格鲁 (Thomas Killigrew) 的要求于 1660 年代初建造的，当时剧院在英国复辟期间被允许重新开放。它最初被称为“布里奇斯街皇家剧院”。剧院的业主聘请了定期在剧院演出的著名演员，包括内尔·格温和查尔斯·哈特。 1672年，剧院着火，基利格鲁在同一地块上建造了一座更大的剧院，更名为“德鲁里巷皇家剧院”，并于1674年开业。这座建筑在科利·西伯尔、大卫·加里克和理查德·布林斯利·谢里登的领导下持续了近120年，其中最后一位雇用了约瑟夫·格里马尔迪作为剧院的常驻小丑。 1791 年，在谢里登的管理下，该建筑被拆除，以便为 1794 年开业的更大剧院腾出空间。这条新德鲁里巷在 1809 年被烧毁之前幸存了 15 年。今天矗立的建筑于 1812 年开业。这里曾是包括埃德在内的知名演员的居住地

The house is made with extraordinary good contrivance, and yet hath some faults, as the narrowness of the passages in and out of the Pitt, and the distance from the stage to the boxes, which I am confident cannot hear; but for all other things it is well, only, above all, the musique being below, and most of it sounding under the very stage, there is no hearing of the bases at all, nor very well of the trebles, which sure must be mended. Performances usually began at 3 pm to take advantage of the daylight: the main floor for the audience, the pit, had no roof in order to let in the light. A glazed dome was built over the opening, but according to one of Pepys' diary entries, the dome was not entirely effective at keeping out the elements: he and his wife were forced to leave the theatre to take refuge from a hail storm. Green baize cloth covered the benches in the pit and served to decorate the boxes (additionally ornamented with gold-tooled leather) and even the stage itself. The backless green benches in the pit were in a semicircular arrangement facing the stage, according to a May 1663 letter from one Monsieur de Maonconys: "All benches of the pit, where people of rank also sit, are shaped in a semi-circle, each row higher than the next." The three galleries formed a semicircle around the floor seats; both the first and second galleries were divided up into boxes.
