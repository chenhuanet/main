1951年5月14日：泰爾依鐵路May 14, 1951: Talyllyn Railway

泰尔依铁道（英语：Talyllyn Railway，威尔士语：Rheilffordd Talyllyn）是英国的一条窄轨观光铁路，由泰尔依至Nant Gwernol，全长12公里（7.25英里）。本路于1866年开始营运，将Bryn Eglwys的板岩运到泰尔依，是获英国国会法令授权建造的第一条以蒸汽动力载客的窄轨铁路。虽然铁路投资严重不足，仍然得以维持营运，并在1951年起成为世界第一条由志工运作的观光铁路。 转型为观光铁路后本路持续努力吸引观光客，购入机车与客车，并在1976年，沿着旧运矿路线，将路线由bergynolwyn延伸到Nant Gwernol的新站。2001年，保留铁路的团体庆祝50周年纪念。2005年开始了大型重建计划与往Tywyn码头站的延伸，窄轨铁路博物馆也进行扩充。

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「1951年5月14日：泰爾依鐵路」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

### 补记

还需要补上一层：历史写作最难的地方，不是把事件说得热闹，而是把它的尺度说准。一个日期之所以能进入“历史上的今天”，往往因为它同时具备可被记录的动作、可被辨认的现场和可被后来者继续追问的后果。照片会褪色，报纸会变黄，建筑会被改建，但这些问题不会自动消失。它们会在城市更新、技术迭代和公共记忆的争论中一次次返回。也正因如此，重读这一天，应当同时保留两种眼光：一种贴近现场，看见具体的人在具体的时间做出具体的选择；另一种拉远距离，看见这些选择如何被制度、市场和偶然性放大。两种眼光互相校正，历史才不至于变成单纯的赞歌或单纯的清算。我们把这些图像放在一起，是希望读者先看见，再判断；先理解，再纪念。

The Talyllyn Railway (Welsh: Rheilffordd Talyllyn) is a narrow-gauge railway in Wales, which runs for 7+1⁄4 miles (12 km) from Tywyn on the Mid-Wales coast to Nant Gwernol near the village of Abergynolwyn. Opened in 1865 to carry slate from the quarries at Bryn Eglwys to Tywyn, it was the first narrow-gauge railway in Britain authorised by Act of Parliament to carry passengers using steam haulage. Despite severe underinvestment, the line remained open, and in 1951 it became the first railway in the world to be preserved as a heritage railway by volunteers. Since preservation, the railway has operated as a tourist attraction, expanding its rolling stock through acquisition and an engineering programme to build new locomotives and carriages. In 1976, an extension was opened along the former mineral line from Abergynolwyn to the new station at Nant Gwernol.

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

Rev. W Awdry儿童铁路系列之一的小说“Skarloey Railway”，就是依据泰尔依铁路所写。本路的保留也启发了Ealing Comedy的一出戏：“The Titfield Thunderbolt”。 铁路名字的由来并不清楚，可能是泰尔依教区（本路东侧终点站所在地），或是 Tal-y-llyn（Tal-y-llyn是 Cadair Idris 东方4.8 公里以东的带状冰川湖）。本路轨距为少见的 686 毫米，英国只有其他三家铁路也使用该轨距：成立较泰尔依为早的 Corris Railway，之后成立的 Plynlimon and Hafan Tramway ，以及 Campbeltown and Machrihanish Light Railway。

The fictional Skarloey Railway, which formed part of The Railway Series of children's books by the Rev. W. Awdry, was based on the Talyllyn Railway. The preservation of the line inspired the Ealing Comedy film The Titfield Thunderbolt. The origin of the railway's name is uncertain: it may refer to the parish of Tal-y-llyn, which contains its eastern terminus, or it may come from Tal-y-llyn, a large glacial ribbon lake at the foot of Cadair Idris 3 miles (4.8 km) further east. The 2 ft 3 in (686 mm) gauge of the track is unusual, and was shared by only three other public railways in the United Kingdom: the Corris Railway and the Plynlimon and Hafan Tramway both a few miles from the Talyllyn, and the Campbeltown and Machrihanish Light Railway in Scotland. Only the Corris Railway is still in operation.

第 2 章Chapter 2

现场The event

19世纪30年代，板岩开采在 Tywyn 上面的山头开展。虽然有很多小矿场和试掘点，但是在该区只有位于 Bryn Eglwys（城镇东北面11公里）的主矿场。19世纪40年代早期开始地下的挖掘。1847年，矿场由本地地主 John Pughe 运作。完成的板岩由驮马运送到 Pennal 码头，装船河运至 Aberdyfi（又名 Aberdovey），最后再装载到船只海运。如此繁杂而昂贵的运输限制了矿场的产出。1861年，美国内战爆发，英格兰西北一带的工坊失去了棉花的供应。不少富有的主人开始寻找新的商机。兰开夏的 William McConnel 就是一员。1859年，他在 Tywyn 以北的 Dolgellau 附近购买房屋。1864年1月，McConnel 组成 Aberdovey Slate Company ，租得土地，并从Machynlleth 的地主 Lewis Morris 租得 Bryn Eglwys。

Origins and construction: up to 1866 Slate quarrying began in the hills above Tywyn in the 1830s, but although many small quarries and test levels were established, only one major quarry was developed in the region, the Bryn Eglwys quarry, 7 miles (11 km) north-east of the town. Underground working began in the early 1840s, and by 1847 the quarry was being worked by local landowner John Pughe. The finished slates were sent by packhorse to the wharf at Pennal, transferred to boats for a river trip to Aberdyfi (also spelled as Aberdovey), and then finally loaded into seagoing vessels, a complex and expensive transportation arrangement which limited the quarry's output. In 1861 the outbreak of the American Civil War cut off supplies of cotton to the mills of the north west of England and as a result a number of prosperous mill owners looked for new business opportunities to diversify their interests. One such owner was William McConnel of Lancashire who, in 1859, had purchased Hengwrt Hall near Dolgellau, north of Tywyn. In January 1864, McConnel formed the Aberdovey Slate Company, which leased the land including Bryn Eglwys from the landowner, Lewis Morris of Machynlleth.

第 3 章Chapter 3

余波Aftermath

McConnel 着手改进 Bryn Eglwys ，以增加产出。1865年，他的公司筹资建造窄轨铁路，连接矿场与 Aberdyfi 港。由于标准轨距的 Aberystwyth and Welsh Coast Railway 很快地在1863年从 Machynlleth建抵 Tywyn。 McConnel 决定建造矿场至 Tywyn 的路线，这是矿场的石板运抵标准轨铁路的最短路线。但 Afon Dyfi 河口和南面衔接，困难重重，支线本来孤立于系统的其它部分。国会法令(28 and 29 Vict, cap cccxv) 于1865年7月5日，容许公司可以进行客运 。公司任命 James Swinton Spooner 为负责的工程师。他计划的路向相对笔直，由 Tywyn 稳定地爬升至矿场。兴建工作很快开始。1866年9月，工程进展理想，商务部的视察员 Captain Henry Tyler ，可初步视察并汇报。

McConnel set about improving Bryn Eglwys to increase its output. He focused on providing rail transport for the isolated quarry, and in April 1864 he reached an agreement with local landowners to purchase the land necessary to build a railway towards Tywyn and onwards to the port of Aberdyfi. Construction was well underway by July 1864. The standard gauge Aberystwith and Welsh Coast Railway was expanding rapidly from its base at Machynlleth, however, and in 1863 had reached Tywyn, so McConnel decided to build his line from the quarry to Tywyn, as the nearest point where slate could be transferred to the standard gauge railway. This was despite the line's initial isolation from the rest of the system because of difficulties in bridging the estuary of the Afon Dyfi to the south. An act of Parliament, the Talyllyn Railway Act 1865 (28 & 29 Vict. c. cccxv) allowing the company to operate passenger trains as a public railway, was given royal assent on 5 July 1865, and the company appointed James Swinton Spooner as engineer for the construction. He laid out plans for a relatively straight line climbing steadily from Tywyn to the quarry and work quickly got underway. By September 1866 construction had advanced to the point where the Board of Trade inspector Captain Henry Tyler could make an initial inspection and report.
