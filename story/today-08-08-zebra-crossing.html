斑马线（英语：zebra crossing）是一种髹有黑白间条纹的人行横道，部分地方会在两旁髹有之字形白线，并附设卑利沙灯。斑马线由道路研究实验室交通运输部首任负责人、物理学家兼交通工程师乔治·查尔斯沃思（George Charlesworth）设计，首道斑马线位于英国斯劳高街（High Street），而斑马线现见于英国、澳大利亚、新西兰、香港等地。斑马线一般给予行人优先通行权，而侵犯行人在斑马线上的优先通行权的驾驶者在不同地方均会被处罚。 斑马线的“斑马”之名出自何处有所争议。一般认为“斑马”之名源自英国国会议员、后来于1976年4月5日就任首相的詹姆斯·詹姆斯·卡拉汉于1948年到访当时正在研究全新的安全人行横道的道路研究实验室（Road Research Laboratory）之事，在运输研究实验室向詹姆斯·卡拉汉展示黑白间条纹的设计后，詹姆斯·卡拉汉据称曾评论该设计形似斑马，然而詹姆斯·卡拉汉本人并未主张自己为“斑马”之名的作者。

A zebra crossing (British English) or a marked crosswalk (American English) is a pedestrian crossing marked with white stripes (zebra markings). Normally, pedestrians are afforded precedence over vehicular traffic, although the significance of the markings may vary by jurisdiction. The first zebra crossing in the world was installed in Slough, United Kingdom, in 1951 to enhance pedestrian safety at new and already existing crossing points. Since then, zebra markings have come to be used internationally to denote pedestrian crossings, though many have been replaced by various types of signalised crossing due to safety concerns.

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

英国自1930年代起已根据《1934年道路交通法令》第18条引入附设卑利沙灯的人行横道。卑利沙灯得名自1934年至1937年出任交通大臣的卑利沙勋爵，是位于黑白间条高柱上的黄色球状灯号，至今在英国仍须设置于斑马线与平行过路处（parallel crossing）旁。 然而，车流量的增加使卑利沙灯的有效性逐渐减弱，行人与驾驶者均开始忽略人行横道。1949年至1951年间，当时的交通运输部曾尝试各种设计改善人行横道的可见度与增加人行横道的使用率，直至引入黑白间条纹的斑马线为止，其时斑马线已在英国各地的1000个人行横道进行试验。斑马线由道路研究实验室交通运输部首任负责人、物理学家兼交通工程师乔治·查尔斯沃思设计。首道斑马线于1951年10月31日在斯劳高街（High Street）引入。 在英国，斑马线起初属于未有划线的人行横道，后来划上白色线，使斑马线与卑利沙灯灯柱的颜色相呼应。斑马线的两旁均髹有之字形白线，以确保驾驶者与行人能清楚看见彼此，而驾驶者亦被禁止在髹有之字形白线的控制区内停车。英国法律规定包括驾驶者在内的所有道路使用者必须让路予已踏上斑马线的行人，英国的《公路守则》也有相同的说明。违反该法律规定者会被罚款100英镑，并被扣3分。相较于其他执行最高2000英镑罚款的国家相比，违反该法律规定的处罚因过于宽大而引发批评。然而，道路使用者如果未有在由学校交通安全队（school crossing patrol）指挥交通的斑马线让路予已踏上斑马线的行人，最高刑罚则提升为罚款1000英镑与被扣至少3分，并有可能被取消驾驶执照。英国的驾驶者即使并非位于斑马线上，只要遇到学校交通安全队就必须停车。

Terminology and usage of the markings varies by country. In the UK and other Commonwealth countries, they are usually called zebra crossings, as the stripes resemble the striped coat of a zebra. In the UK and some other European countries, zebra markings are only found at unsignalised, standalone crossings without traffic signals and must be accompanied with upright belisha beacons; in this context, the striped markings indicate absolute priority for pedestrians. In North America and in much of Continental Europe, they can be found at any type of crossing; in this context, zebra markings may or may not indicate pedestrian priority, and this priority may be dependent on traffic signals. The origin of the zebra title is debated. It is generally attributed to British MP James Callaghan who, in 1948, visited the country's Transport and Road Research Laboratory which was working on a new idea for safe pedestrian crossings. On being shown a black and white design, Callaghan is said to have remarked that it resembled a zebra. Callaghan did not himself claim authorship of the term. The first zebra crossing was introduced on Slough High Street in the United Kingdom on 31 October 1951. Pedestrian crossings with Belisha beacons had been in use in the UK since the 1930s, originally introduced under section 18 of the Road Traffic Act 1934. The Belisha beacon is an upright crossing marking, still required at zebra and parallel crossings in the UK, named after the Minister of Transport in 1934, Leslie Hore-Belisha.

第 2 章Chapter 2

现场The event

在澳大利亚，与英国法律的规定相同，作为国家统一立法（National uniform legislation）之一的《澳大利亚道路规则》（Australian Road Rules）规定驾驶者必须让路予已踏上斑马线的行人，然而由于澳大利亚实行联邦制，澳大利亚各州与领地对违反该法律规定者的罚则有所不同，比如新南威尔士州与昆士兰州的罚则均为最高扣20分，而维多利亚州的罚则则为扣5分。此外，澳大利亚存在在路中央隆起的斑马线，此类斑马线别称“袋熊线”（wombat crossing）。 在新西兰，与英国法律的规定相同，《2004年陆路运输（道路使用者）规则》（Land Transport (Road User) Rule 2004）规定驾驶者必须让路予已踏上斑马线的行人，违反该法律规定者根据《1999年陆路运输（罪行及罚则）规例》（Land Transport (Offences and Penalties) Regulations 1999）最高可被罚款1000新西兰元与被征收150新西兰元的违规费用，并被扣35分。

However, with an increase of car traffic, the effectiveness of the beacons was waning; both pedestrians and drivers were ignoring the crossing. From 1949 to 1951, the then-named Ministry of Transport experimented with designs to improve visibility and increase usage, until the familiar black and white stripes were introduced. The zebra crossing was then trialed at 1,000 experimental sites across the UK at this time. The zebra markings are credited to physicist and traffic engineer George Charlesworth, who was the first head of the traffic section at the Road Research Laboratory. The crossing is characterized by longitudinal stripes on the road, parallel to the flow of the traffic, alternately a light colour and a dark one. The similarity of these markings to those of a zebra gave rise to the crossing's name. The light colour is usually white and the dark colour may be painted – in which case black is typical – or left unpainted if the road surface itself is dark. The stripes are typically 40–60 cm (16–24 in) wide.

第 3 章Chapter 3

余波Aftermath

英属香港政府在1950年代将斑马线连同卑利沙灯引入香港，以取代原有的“绅士脚”人行横道标志，并改善行人交通安全，警务处交通部更于1958年推出了卡通角色“斑马佬”鼓励市民以斑马线横过马路。与英国的斑马线相同，香港的斑马线的两旁均髹有之字形白线，以确保驾驶者与行人能清楚看见彼此，而驾驶者亦被禁止在髹有之字形白线的控制区内停车。与英国法律的规定相同，香港法例第374G章《道路交通（交通管制）规例》规定包括驾驶者在内的所有道路使用者必须让路予已踏上斑马线的行人，违反该法律规定者如属首次被定罪，可处以第2级罚款及监禁3个月，否则则可处以第3级罚款及监禁6个月，第2级罚款与第3级罚款的金额在香港法例第221章《刑事诉讼程序条例》中被分别指定为5000港元与1万港元。 英国伦敦阿比路上的斑马线因披头士乐队的专辑《阿比路》的封面在该处取景而为人所知。该专辑使该斑马线成为旅游景点，阿比路录音室的标志也因而包含了该斑马线的元素。在拍摄该专辑的封面图像后，英国的所有斑马线均在路边与路中央补上之字形白线。该斑马线亦被英格兰遗产委员会列为二级登录建筑。

Sometimes, zebra crossings are placed on a speed bump, meaning the zebra crossing is level with the pavement. This is done to make it safer for pedestrians to cross, since drivers need to slow down to go over the speed bump. However, this is more expensive than a traditional zebra crossing, and can impede the flow of traffic and response times for emergency vehicles, especially on roads with higher speed limits. In the United Kingdom, the crossing is marked with Belisha beacons, which are flashing amber globes on black and white posts on each side of the road, named after Leslie Hore-Belisha, the Minister of Transport, who introduced them in 1934. Pedestrians have priority when they step onto the crossing: The Highway Code states that road traffic "MUST give way when a pedestrian has moved onto a crossing."
