1937年10月5日：牛津大学王后学院October 5, 1937: The Queen's College, Oxford

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「1937年10月5日：牛津大学王后学院」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

### 补记

还需要补上一层：历史写作最难的地方，不是把事件说得热闹，而是把它的尺度说准。一个日期之所以能进入“历史上的今天”，往往因为它同时具备可被记录的动作、可被辨认的现场和可被后来者继续追问的后果。照片会褪色，报纸会变黄，建筑会被改建，但这些问题不会自动消失。它们会在城市更新、技术迭代和公共记忆的争论中一次次返回。也正因如此，重读这一天，应当同时保留两种眼光：一种贴近现场，看见具体的人在具体的时间做出具体的选择；另一种拉远距离，看见这些选择如何被制度、市场和偶然性放大。两种眼光互相校正，历史才不至于变成单纯的赞歌或单纯的清算。我们把这些图像放在一起，是希望读者先看见，再判断；先理解，再纪念。

The Queen's College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford, England. The college was founded in 1341 by Robert de Eglesfield in honour of Philippa of Hainault, queen of England. It is distinguished by its predominantly neoclassical architecture, primarily dating from the 18th century. As of 2022, the college had an endowment of £327.8 million making it the fifth-wealthiest Oxford college (after Christ Church, Magdalen, St. John's, and All Souls).

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

Virtual Tour of the Queen's College （页面存档备份，存于互联网档案馆）

The college was founded in 1341 as "Hall of the Queen's scholars of Oxford" by Robert de Eglesfield (d'Eglesfield), chaplain to the then queen consort Philippa of Hainault, after whom the hall was named. The queen consort continues to be the patroness of the college by tradition. Robert's aim was to provide clergymen for his native Cumberland and where he lived in Westmorland (both part of modern Cumbria). In addition, the college was to provide charity for the poor. The college's coat of arms is that of the founder; it differs slightly from his family's coat of arms, which did not include the gold star on the breast of the first eagle. The current coat of arms was adopted by d'Eglesfield because he was unable to use his family's arms, being the younger son. D'Eglesfield had grand plans for the college, with a provost, 12 fellows studying theology, up to 13 chaplains, and 72 poor boys. However, the college did not have the funding to support such numbers, and initially had just two fellows. The college gained land and patronage in the mid-15th century, giving it a good endowment and allowing it to expand to 10 fellows by the end of the century. By 1500, the college had started to take paying undergraduates, typically sons of the gentry and middle class, who paid the fellows for teaching. There were 14 of these in 1535; by 1612, this had risen to 194. The college added lectureships in Greek and philosophy. 现场The event

Queen's College JCR （页面存档备份，存于互联网档案馆）

From the 1750s, as with other Oxford colleges, standards dropped. The Oxford commission of 1850–1859 revised the statutes and removed the northern preference for fellows and most of the students. Over the coming years, requirements for fellows to be unmarried were relaxed, the number of fellows required to have taken orders and studied theology was reduced, and the Universities Tests Act 1871 allowed non-conformists and Catholics. Like many of Oxford's colleges, Queen's admitted its first mixed-sex cohort in 1979, after more than six centuries as an institution for men only.

The college is named for its first patroness, Queen Philippa. Established in January 1341 'under the name of the Hall of the Queen's scholars of Oxford' (sub nomine aule scholarium Regine de Oxon), the college was subsequently called the 'Queen's Hall', 'Queenhall' and 'Queen's College'. The Queen's College, Oxford Act 1584 (27 Eliz. 1. c. 2 Pr.) sought to end this confusion by providing that it should be called by the one name "the Queen's College";. The full name of the college, as indicated in its annual reports, is The Provost and Scholars of The Queen's College in the University of Oxford. Queens' College in Cambridge positions its apostrophe differently and has no article, as it was named for multiple queens (Margaret of Anjou and Elizabeth Woodville). In April 2012, as part of the celebrations of the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II, a series of commemorative stamps was released featuring A-Z pictures of famous British landmarks. The Queen's College's front quad was used on the Q stamp, alongside other landmarks such as the Angel of the North on A and the Old Bailey on O.
