芝加哥剧院（英语：Chicago Theatre），旧称为巴拉班和卡茨芝加哥剧院（英语：Balaban and Katz Chicago Theatre），是位于美国伊利诺伊州芝加哥卢普区州街上的一座剧院和地标性建筑物。剧院建造于1921年，当时乃由A. J. Balaban和Barney Balaban兄弟及其合伙人Sam Katz 所成立的巴拉班和卡茨剧院（Balaban and Katz，B&K）旗下的旗舰剧场。1925至1945年间，芝加哥剧院与巴拉班和卡茨旗下其他剧院垄断了当时的剧院市场。现在，芝加哥剧院由麦迪逊广场花园公司拥有及营运，并作为一个热门的表演艺术场地，可用作表演话剧、舞蹈、歌剧、音乐剧、魔术、杂技、演讲等项目。 芝加哥剧院的建筑物于1979年6月6日被纳入美国国家史迹名录，并于1983年1月28日被官方列为芝加哥地标。独特的芝加哥剧院大门罩是“城市的非正式象征”，经常出现在电影、电视、艺术品和摄影作品中。

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「1921年10月26日：芝加哥劇院」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

### 补记

还需要补上一层：历史写作最难的地方，不是把事件说得热闹，而是把它的尺度说准。一个日期之所以能进入“历史上的今天”，往往因为它同时具备可被记录的动作、可被辨认的现场和可被后来者继续追问的后果。照片会褪色，报纸会变黄，建筑会被改建，但这些问题不会自动消失。它们会在城市更新、技术迭代和公共记忆的争论中一次次返回。也正因如此，重读这一天，应当同时保留两种眼光：一种贴近现场，看见具体的人在具体的时间做出具体的选择；另一种拉远距离，看见这些选择如何被制度、市场和偶然性放大。两种眼光互相校正，历史才不至于变成单纯的赞歌或单纯的清算。我们把这些图像放在一起，是希望读者先看见，再判断；先理解，再纪念。

The Chicago Theatre, originally known as the Balaban and Katz Chicago Theatre, is a theater on North State Street in the Loop area of Chicago, Illinois. Built in 1921, the Chicago Theatre was the flagship for the Balaban and Katz (B&K) group of theaters run by A. J. Balaban, his brother Barney Balaban and partner Sam Katz. Along with the other B&K theaters, from 1925 to 1945 the Chicago Theatre was a dominant movie theater enterprise. Currently, Madison Square Garden, Inc. owns and operates the Chicago Theatre as a 3600 seat performing arts venue for stage plays, magic shows, comedy, speeches, sporting events and popular music concerts. The building was added to the National Register of Historic Places on June 6, 1979, and was listed as a Chicago Landmark on January 28, 1983.

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

芝加哥剧院（英语：Chicago Theatre），旧称为巴拉班和卡茨芝加哥剧院（英语：Balaban and Katz Chicago Theatre），是位于美国伊利诺伊州芝加哥卢普区州街上的一座剧院和地标性建筑物。剧院建造于1921年，当时乃由A. J. Balaban和Barney Balaban兄弟及其合伙人Sam Katz 所成立的巴拉班和卡茨剧院（Balaban and Katz，B&K）旗下的旗舰剧场。1925至1945年间，芝加哥剧院与巴拉班和卡茨旗下其他剧院垄断了当时的剧院市场。现在，芝加哥剧院由麦迪逊广场花园公司拥有及营运，并作为一个热门的表演艺术场地，可用作表演话剧、舞蹈、歌剧、音乐剧、魔术、杂技、演讲等项目。

Abe and Barney Balaban, together with Sam and Morris Katz—founders of the Balaban and Katz theater chain, built the Chicago Theatre in 1921 as one of a large chain of opulent motion picture houses. The theater would become the flagship for 28 theaters in the city and over 100 others in the Midwestern United States that B&K operated in conjunction with the Paramount Publix chain. Cornelius W. Rapp and George L. Rapp were primary architects and the final construction cost was $4 million ($72.2 million in 2025 dollars). The Rapp brothers also designed many other B&K properties in Chicago, including the Oriental and Uptown Theatres. Preceded by the now-demolished Tivoli Theatre of Chicago and Capitol Theatre of New York City, the Chicago Theatre was the "...largest, most costly and grandest of the super deluxe movie palaces" built up to that date and thus now the oldest surviving grand movie palace. The Chicago Theatre was among the earliest theaters in the nation to be built in Rapp and Rapp's signature Neo-Baroque French-revival style. It is the oldest surviving example of this style in Chicago. The original 1921 interior decoration of the auditorium included fourteen large romantic French-themed murals surrounding the proscenium by Chicago artist Louis Grell (1887–1960), a common feature that Rapp and Rapp architects included in their movie palace designs. When it opened October 26, 1921, the 3,880-seat theater was promoted as the "Wonder Theatre of the World".

第 2 章Chapter 2

现场The event

芝加哥剧院的建筑物于1979年6月6日被纳入美国国家史迹名录，并于1983年1月28日被官方列为芝加哥地标。独特的芝加哥剧院大门罩是“城市的非正式象征”，经常出现在电影、电视、艺术品和摄影作品中。

During its first 40 years of operation, the Chicago Theatre presented premiere films and live entertainment. Throughout its existence, many of the top performers and stars of their day made live appearances at the theater. One of its biggest draws was live jazz, which Balaban and Katz promoted as early as September 1922 in a special event they called "Syncopation Week". This proved so successful that jazz bands became a mainstay of the Chicago Theatre's programming through the 1920s and into the 1930s. In preparation for the 1933 World's Fair in Chicago, the Chicago Theatre was redecorated. Part of the World's Fair renovation included another commission by Balaban & Katz for Grell to repaint the architecturally enclosed fourteen murals. This time Grell chose Greek/Roman deities as the theme for the large oil on canvas murals which are on public exhibit today in the theatre auditorium. The building has been associated with popular culture occasions. For example, Ronald Reagan announced his engagement to Jane Wyman at the theater. Another modernization occurred in the 1950s when management discontinued stage shows. During the economic and social changes of the 1970s, business at the theatre slowed for owner Plitt Theatres, affecting ongoing viability.

第 3 章Chapter 3

余波Aftermath

芝加哥剧院的建筑物于1979年6月6日被纳入美国国家史迹名录，并于1983年1月28日被官方列为芝加哥地标。独特的芝加哥剧院大门罩是“城市的非正式象征”，经常出现在电影、电视、艺术品和摄影作品中。

The Chicago Theatre Preservation Group commenced renovation of the buildings which were completed in 1986 at a cost of $9 million ($26.4 million), with $4.3 million ($12.6 million) spent on the Theatre. The renovation by architects Daniel P. Coffey & Associates, Ltd. and interior design consultants A.T. Heinsbergen & Co. restored the Chicago Theatre to a 1930s appearance and a seating capacity of 3,600. The theatre reopened September 10, 1986, with a performance by Frank Sinatra marking the culmination of a four-year historic preservation effort championed by the Landmarks Preservation Council of Illinois, The gala reopening was also symbolic because Sinatra had performed at the theater in the 1950s. The restoration of the adjoining Page Building, itself a Chicago and National Register landmark, provided office space to support the theatre. The theater, like its neighbor the Joffrey Tower, is an important component of the North Loop/Theatre District revitalization plan. Theatre district revitalization plans go back as far as Mayor Jane Byrne's 1981 plan. On April 1, 2004, TheatreDreams Chicago, LLC purchased the building for $3 million. The Balaban and Katz trademark is now the property of the Balaban and Katz Historical Foundation. New York's Madison Square Garden Entertainment announced October 11, 2007, that it would buy the theater.
