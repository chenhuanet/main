2000年11月2日：遠征1November 2, 2000: Expedition 1

远征1是历史上第一次到国际空间站的远征队。Expedition 1 was the first long-duration expedition to the International Space Station (ISS).

威廉·薛佛（William Shepherd，NASA，曾执行4次飞行），指令长 谢尔盖·克里卡列夫（Sergei K. Krikalev，RSA，曾执行5次飞行），飞行工程师

## 史料补记

### 一、时代条件

把「2000年11月2日：遠征1」放回它发生的年代，会发现这一天并不是孤立的新闻标题，而是一连串技术条件、城市生活与个人选择交汇后的结果。历史照片之所以重要，不在于它替我们给出结论，而在于它把当时的光线、材料、人物距离和现场秩序保留下来。档案里的每一张底片、每一次曝光和每一行手写说明，都在提醒后来者：事件发生的那一刻，世界并不知道自己将被如何讲述。对于研究者来说，首先要把事件还原到它自己的时间尺度里，理解当时可用的工具、可以动员的人力和尚未被命名的风险，然后再讨论它在更长历史中的位置。

### 二、影像现场

从影像史的角度看，这类事件往往同时留下两种痕迹。一种是正式的记录：报纸头版、机构公报、工程图纸和庆典合影，它们把事件整理成可供引用的事实；另一种是边缘的痕迹：临时拍下的街景、未及刊出的照片、参与者留下的笔记和后来被反复翻印的明信片。两者之间常有缝隙，而缝隙正是历史研究最有价值的地方。我们在这一天重看这些图像，不是为了把过去变成简单的纪念，而是为了理解当时的人如何在有限的信息中做出判断：哪些细节被保留，哪些声音被剪掉，哪些面孔被安排在画面的中央。

### 三、后续回声

对今天的读者而言，这个日期的意义还在于它与更长的历史脉络相连。城市在事件之后改变了自己的道路与天际线，技术在被使用之后才逐渐显出后果，普通人的日常也在新闻退潮后继续。把故事讲到这一层，图片就不再只是插图，而是证据、情绪和时间的容器。它让我们看到：所谓“历史上的今天”，既是某一天的回声，也是今天仍在继续的问题。重访这一天，不是为了替过去盖棺定论，而是为了把那些仍然影响当下的选择、代价与记忆，重新放到可以讨论的位置。

### 补记

还需要补上一层：历史写作最难的地方，不是把事件说得热闹，而是把它的尺度说准。一个日期之所以能进入“历史上的今天”，往往因为它同时具备可被记录的动作、可被辨认的现场和可被后来者继续追问的后果。照片会褪色，报纸会变黄，建筑会被改建，但这些问题不会自动消失。它们会在城市更新、技术迭代和公共记忆的争论中一次次返回。也正因如此，重读这一天，应当同时保留两种眼光：一种贴近现场，看见具体的人在具体的时间做出具体的选择；另一种拉远距离，看见这些选择如何被制度、市场和偶然性放大。两种眼光互相校正，历史才不至于变成单纯的赞歌或单纯的清算。我们把这些图像放在一起，是希望读者先看见，再判断；先理解，再纪念。

Expedition 1 was the first long-duration expedition to the International Space Station (ISS). The three-person crew stayed aboard the station for 136 days, from 2 November 2000 to 19 March 2001. It was the beginning of an uninterrupted human presence on the station which continues as of 2026. The official start of the expedition occurred when the crew docked to the station on 2 November 2000, aboard the Russian spacecraft Soyuz TM-31, which had launched on 31 October 2000 at the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan. During their mission, the Expedition 1 crew activated various systems on board the station, unpacked equipment that had been delivered, and hosted three visiting Space Shuttle crews and two uncrewed Russian Progress resupply vehicles. The crew was very busy throughout the mission, which was declared a success.

第 1 章Chapter 1

缘起Origins

尤里·吉德津科（Yuri Gidzenko，RSA，曾执行2次飞行），飞行工程师

The three visiting Space Shuttles brought equipment, supplies, and key components of the space station. The first of these, STS-97, docked in early December 2000, and brought the first pair of large U.S. photovoltaic arrays, which increased the station's power capabilities fivefold. The second visiting shuttle mission was STS-98, which was docked in mid-February 2001 and delivered the US$1.4 billion research module Destiny, which increased the mass of the station beyond that of Mir for the first time. Mid-March 2001 saw the final shuttle visit of the expedition, STS-102, whose main purpose was to exchange the Expedition 1 crew with the next three-person long-duration crew, Expedition 2. The expedition ended when Discovery undocked from the station on 18 March 2001. The Expedition 1 crew consisted of an American commander and two Russians. The commander, Bill Shepherd, had been in space three times before, all on shuttle missions which lasted at most a week. The Russians, Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei K. Krikalev, both had previous long-duration spaceflights on Mir, with Krikalev having spent over a year in space. The commander, Bill Shepherd, was a former Navy SEAL, whose only spaceflights were on shuttle missions, and at the beginning of the mission his total time in space was about two weeks. Questions had been raised by the Russian space agency about the choice of Shepherd as mission commander due to his lack of experience. Flight engineer Sergei Krikalev had spent over a year in orbit, mostly on Mir, and would become the first person to visit the ISS twice.

第 2 章Chapter 2

现场The event

尤里·吉德津科（Yuri Gidzenko，RSA，曾执行2次飞行），飞行工程师

Shepherd was only the second U.S. astronaut to be launched in a Russian spacecraft, the first being Norman Thagard, who launched on Soyuz TM-21 to visit Mir in 1995. Shepherd expected one of the biggest challenges for the ISS would be the compatibility of technologies between Russian and U.S. The first component of the International Space Station was the Zarya module, which was launched uncrewed on 20 November 1998. Between this launch and the arrival of the first crew, five crewed Space Shuttle missions from the U.S. and two uncrewed Russian flights expanded the station and prepared it for human habitation.

第 3 章Chapter 3

余波Aftermath

尤里·吉德津科（Yuri Gidzenko，RSA，曾执行2次飞行），飞行工程师 档案补记：探险 1 是第一次对国际空间站 (ISS) 的长期探险。从2000年11月2日到2001年3月19日，这三名宇航员在空间站上停留了136天。这是人类在空间站上不间断存在的开始，一直持续到2026年。当宇航员们于2000年11月2日搭乘俄罗斯航天器联盟号TM-31停靠在空间站时，探险正式开始，该航天器于2000年10月31日在哈萨克斯坦的拜科努尔航天发射场。在执行任务期间，远征一号机组人员激活了空间站上的各种系统，打开了已交付的设备，并接待了三名来访的航天飞机机组人员和两辆无人驾驶的俄罗斯进步号补给车。整个任务期间，机组人员非常忙碌，任务被宣布为成功。三架来访的航天飞机带来了空间站的设备、物资和关键部件。其中第一个 STS-97 于 2000 年 12 月上旬对接，并带来了美国第一对大型光伏阵列，使该站的电力能力增加了五倍。第二次访问航天飞机任务是 STS-98，它于 2001 年 2 月中旬对接并交付了价值 14 亿美元的研究舱“命运”，该太空站的质量首次超过了和平号。 2001 年 3 月中旬，探险队 STS-102 进行了最后一次航天飞机访问，其主要目的是将探险 1 号机组人员与接下来的三人长期机组人员探险 2 进行交换。当发现号于 2001 年 3 月 18 日离开空间站时，探险结束。探险 1 号机组人员包括一名美国指挥官和两名俄罗斯人。指挥官比尔·谢泼德 (Bill Shepherd) 此前曾三次进入太空，每次都执行航天飞机任务，最多持续一小时

Four of the five Shuttle flights focused primarily on delivering and unpacking substantial cargo for future crews. STS-88 launched on 4 December 1998, delivered the Unity module, the first component of the US Orbital Segment (USOS) during the mission, the crew spent several hours unpacking and setting up both Unity and Zarya. Krikalev, who would be a Flight Engineer on Expedition 1 flew on STS-88 and together with Shuttle Commander Robert D. Cabana became the first two people to jointly enter Unity and later Zarya. STS-96, in May 1999, delivered 1,618 kilograms (3,567 lb) of pressurized cargo, 320 liters (84 U.S. gal) of water, and 300 kilograms (662 lb) of unpressurized cargo that was attached during a nearly eight hour EVA. Space Shuttle Atlantis visited the station during the STS-101 mission in May 2000. It delivered another 1,500 kilograms (3,300 lb) of pressurized cargo, and performed station maintenance. Near the end of the mission, they used Atlantis's thrusters to boost the ISS by 42 kilometres (26 mi) into a 380 kilometres (236 mi) orbit. Three of STS-101’s crew members — Yury V. Usachev, James S. Voss and Susan J. Helms — would visit the station the following year as the crew of Expedition 2.
