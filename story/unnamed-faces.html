没有留下名字的人The people without names

十二张肖像，和十二种没有被写下的名字Twelve portraits, and twelve names never written down

历史习惯把名字留给重要的人。可照片里绝大多数人没有留下名字：他们只留下姿势、衣服、发型、怀里的孩子、身边的狗。

这个专题不替他们编名字，也不把“未识别”当成失败。它只想把十二张肖像并排放在一起，看看在没有名字的地方，人还能怎样被看见。

History is used to keeping names for important people. Yet most people in photographs left no name: only a pose, clothes, a hairstyle, a child in their arms, a dog beside them.

This story does not invent names for them, nor treat “unidentified” as a failure. It simply places twelve portraits side by side, to see how people can still be seen where there is no name.

第 1 章Chapter 1

名字没有写下来Names not written down

最早的银版肖像里，名字常常只写在账单上，不写在照片背面。馆藏能确认的是职业、姿势、衣服，常常不是姓名。于是我们只能看见一个人怎样坐着、怎样屏住呼吸，却不知道他叫什么。

In the earliest daguerreotype portraits, names were often written on the bill, not on the back of the plate. What the archive can confirm is occupation, pose and clothing, often not a name. So we see how a person sat and held their breath, without knowing what they were called.

第 2 章Chapter 2

镜头替他们命名The lens names them

当照片进入市场、殖民地和人类学，题名开始替被摄者“命名”：部落、族群、地域、职业。这些词不是名字，却决定了后来的人怎样找到他们。

When photographs entered the market, the colonies and anthropology, titles began to “name” the sitter: tribe, ethnicity, region, occupation. These words were not names, yet they decided how later people would find them.

第 3 章Chapter 3

未识别Unidentified

“Unidentified”不是空白，它是一种历史位置。这些照片里的人穿戴整齐、看着镜头，显然知道自己在被拍。我们能做的，是把“不知道”如实写下来，而不是用想象填满它。

“Unidentified” is not a blank; it is a historical position. The people in these photographs are neatly dressed and looking at the camera, clearly aware they are being photographed. What we can do is write down “we do not know” honestly, rather than fill it with imagination.
