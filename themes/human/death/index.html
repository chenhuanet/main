死亡与记忆Death and Memory

墓葬、墓碑、葬礼、灾难与纪念。死亡不是终点，而是一组需要被安排、被观看、被重复的仪式。Tombs, graves, funerals, disasters and memorials. Death is not an endpoint but a set of rites to be arranged, watched and repeated.

论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

死亡图像不是关于死者，而是关于留下来的人怎样安排记忆。绘画把死亡放进宗教叙事、墓葬空间和王侯纪念碑，让逝者在秩序里继续占据位置；摄影把死亡带到现场，记录葬礼、灾难、墓碑和空掉的房间，让不在场的人被迫看见。两种媒介都处理同一个难题：如何让一个已经消失的人继续被社会承认。并置时要注意，绘画可以慢慢构造哀悼，摄影却常常在事件发生后的几分钟内完成选择；速度不同，伦理也不同。Images of death are less about the dead than about how the living arrange memory. Painting places death inside religious narrative, tomb space and princely monument, so the dead keep a position within order; photography brings death to the site, recording funeral, disaster, gravestone and the emptied room, forcing those absent to look. Both media face one problem: how to keep a vanished person socially acknowledged. The pairing also exposes a difference in speed: painting can construct mourning slowly, while photography often chooses within minutes of an event. Different speed means different ethics.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《圣母之死》》（1395）与《1906：旧金山的地动与火》（1906）放在一起：相隔约 511 年，一边用油画处理“图像如何让逝者继续留在社会里？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Death of the Virgin” (1395) beside “1906: San Francisco shakes and burns” (1906): about 511 years apart, one works through “How does an image keep the dead inside society?” in 油画, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
