论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

复制不是真迹的反面，它常常是图像得以旅行、被记住和被改写的真正方式。绘画通过摹本、工坊版本、版画和印刷把一件作品变成许多可携带的版本；摄影从底片、相册、明信片到数字文件，把复制写进媒介本身。两种媒介都让“原件”变得可疑：当一张画可以被临摹，一张照片可以被无限打印，价值究竟来自材料、作者、第一次曝光，还是来自流通的故事。并置时要看，复制在扩大民主，还是在制造新的稀缺。Reproduction is not the opposite of the original; it is often how an image travels, is remembered and rewritten. Painting turns one work into many portable versions through copy, workshop variant, print and book; photography writes reproduction into the medium itself, from negative and album to postcard and digital file. Both make the original questionable: when a painting can be copied and a photograph printed without end, where does value come from: material, author, first exposure or the story of circulation? The pairing asks whether reproduction expands democracy or manufactures new scarcity.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《秋景中的水牛与男孩》》（1127）与《1842年11月26日：聖母大學》（1842）放在一起：相隔约 715 年，一边用绢本设色处理“复制扩大民主，还是制造新的稀缺？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Buffalo and Boy in Autumnal Landscape” (1127) beside “November 26, 1842: University of Notre Dame” (1842): about 715 years apart, one works through “Does reproduction expand democracy or create new scarcity?” in 绢本设色, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
