太空Space

火箭、卫星、轨道、月面与深空探测器；绘画侧从古代天球图到太空想象。太空图像既是工程记录，也是人类第一次从另一个天体回望自己。Rockets, satellites, orbits, the lunar surface and deep-space probes; on the painting side, celestial spheres and imagined worlds. Space images are engineering records, and the first views of Earth from another world.

论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

太空图像既是工程记录，也是国家竞赛和公众想象。绘画从古代天球、月面和太空想象出发，把未知画成神话、几何或乌托邦；摄影从火箭、卫星、轨道和月面出发，把未知变成可传输的数据和“人类第一次从另一个天体回望自己”的画面。两种媒介都在扩大尺度，但一个靠象征把人放进宇宙，一个靠仪器把宇宙变成可操作的坐标。并置时要问，谁有权进入太空，谁只配在地面观看。Space images are engineering records, national rivalry and public imagination at once. Painting starts from celestial sphere, lunar surface and imagined worlds, turning the unknown into myth, geometry or utopia; photography starts from rocket, satellite, orbit and lunar surface, turning the unknown into transmittable data and the first view of humanity from another body. Both expand scale, one placing people inside the cosmos through symbol, the other turning the cosmos into operable coordinates through instruments. The pairing asks who may enter space and who is left to watch from the ground.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《维纳斯（金星），天球系列第43号》》（1460）与《1902年9月1日：科幻电影》（1902）放在一起：相隔约 442 年，一边用铜版画处理“谁有权进入太空，谁只配在地面观看？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Venus (from the Tarocchi, series A: Firmaments of the Universe, #43)” (1460) beside “September 1, 1902: Science fiction film” (1902): about 442 years apart, one works through “Who may enter space, and who watches from the ground?” in 铜版画, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
