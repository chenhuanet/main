山水Landscape

山、水、园林、沙漠与废墟。自然从来不是背景，它决定人怎么走、怎么停、怎么被看见。Mountains, water, gardens, deserts and ruins. Nature is never background: it decides how people walk, stop and are seen.

论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

自然从来不是背景。绘画用山、水、树、云和道路组织一个可以被行走、凝视和拥有的世界；摄影把风景切成可测量、可印刷、可运输的片段，让遥远的地方进入市场、明信片和领土想象。两种媒介都在把土地变成图像，也都参与了“谁有权命名一片风景”的问题。并置时要看，画面里的路、水、边界和人是让风景变得可进入，还是把原住民、劳动者和所有权一起藏到画框外。Nature is never background. Painting organises a world that can be walked, gazed at and owned through mountain, water, tree, cloud and road; photography cuts landscape into measurable, printable, transportable fragments, bringing distant places into market, postcard and territorial imagination. Both turn land into image and take part in the question of who has the right to name a landscape. The pairing asks whether road, water, border and figure make the view enterable, or hide indigenous people, workers and ownership outside the frame.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《秋季迷雾丛林中的乡村小屋》》（1117）与《1894年6月30日：倫敦塔橋》（1894）放在一起：相隔约 777 年，一边用绢本设色处理“谁有权命名一片风景？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Cottages in a Misty Grove in Autumn” (1117) beside “June 30, 1894: Tower Bridge” (1894): about 777 years apart, one works through “Who has the right to name a landscape?” in 绢本设色, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
