劳动Labour

手、工具、厂房、田野与身体姿势。劳动图像既让劳动者被看见，也常被用来证明秩序、效率和国家的生产力；问题不是“有没有劳动”，而是谁在拍、谁被安排站在哪里。Hands, tools, factory, field and posture. Labour images make workers visible and are often used to prove order, efficiency and national productivity; the question is not whether labour exists but who photographs and who is placed where.

论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

劳动图像看起来最直接：手、工具、厂房、田野和身体。但直接并不等于透明。绘画可以把劳动理想化为丰收、秩序和国家的生产力，也可以让工人、农妇和匠人站到画面的中心；摄影可以记录具体的手势、污渍和疲劳，也可以把劳动安排成宣传照里整齐的队伍。两种媒介都参与定义“什么是正当的劳动”。并置时要看谁在劳动、谁在观看、谁从画面外获利，以及劳动者的脸是否被允许出现。Images of labour look direct: hands, tools, factory, field and body. But directness is not transparency. Painting can idealise labour as harvest, order and national productivity, or bring worker, peasant and artisan to the centre; photography can record a specific gesture, grime and fatigue, or arrange labour into the clean rows of a propaganda photograph. Both media help define what counts as proper work. The pairing asks who labours, who looks, who profits outside the frame, and whether the worker's face is allowed to appear.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《女史箴图》》（400）与《1876年10月4日：得克萨斯农工大学》（1876）放在一起：相隔约 1476 年，一边用绢本设色处理“谁在劳动，谁在观看，谁获利？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Admonitions of the Instructress to the Court Ladies” (400) beside “October 4, 1876: Texas A&M University” (1876): about 1476 years apart, one works through “Who labours, who looks, who profits?” in 绢本设色, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
