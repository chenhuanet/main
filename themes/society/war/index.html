战争War

阵列、火光、逃散、废墟与尸体。战争图像不只是胜利记录，它也保存失败者、平民和见证者。Formation, fire, flight, ruins and bodies. War images are not only victory records; they preserve the defeated, the civilians and the witnesses.

论点：两种媒介怎样回答同一个问题Argument: how two media answer one question

战争图像同时是见证、宣传和纪念。绘画可以把战役画成英雄史诗，也可以把失败、逃亡和尸体留在画面的边缘；摄影让战争进入战壕、废墟和平民的身体，但每一张照片都可能被审查、摆拍或重新配文。两种媒介都不是战争的透明窗口：构图、剪裁、标题和流通渠道都在决定谁被看成英雄，谁被看成代价。把绘画与摄影并置，不是用一方揭穿另一方，而是看战争如何要求图像同时承担动员、证据和哀悼三种互相冲突的任务。War images are witness, propaganda and memorial at once. Painting can turn a battle into heroic epic and leave defeat, flight and corpses at the edge of the frame; photography brings war into trench, ruin and civilian body, but every photograph may be censored, staged or recaptioned. Neither medium is a transparent window: composition, crop, caption and circulation decide who appears as hero and who as cost. Pairing painting and photography does not expose one through the other; it shows how war asks images to carry three conflicting tasks at once: mobilisation, evidence and mourning.

并置练习Pairing exercises4

不是“画得像照片”，而是同一时期两种观看方式如何互相解释；每组都写明为什么放在一起。Not 'painting looks like photography', but how two ways of looking explain each other in the same period; each pairing states why it is together.

01把《《蒙娜丽莎》》（1503）与《1912年1月17日：羅伯特·法爾肯·史考特》（1912）放在一起：相隔约 409 年，一边用油画处理“战争图像何时是见证，何时是宣传？”，另一边用摄影给出另一种回答。先看两幅画面怎样安排人、空间和光，再问谁有权拍摄、谁被安排进画面；差异不在像不像，而在证据与想象的分工。Place “Mona Lisa” (1503) beside “January 17, 1912: Robert Falcon Scott” (1912): about 409 years apart, one works through “When is a war image witness, and when propaganda?” in 油画, while the photograph answers otherwise. Read how each arranges people, space and light, then ask who had the right to photograph and who was placed in the frame. The difference is not likeness but the division between evidence and imagination.
